A serious illness or long recovery affects more than the body. Worry, low mood, loneliness and stress are common, for patients and for the family members caring for them. Paying attention to emotional health is part of good care.

## Signs to watch for

- Losing interest in activities that used to bring enjoyment
- Changes in sleep or appetite
- Withdrawing from friends and family
- Constant worry, irritability or tearfulness
- Not following the care plan, or feeling “what’s the point?”

Depression can slow physical recovery, so these signs are worth raising with your nurse or doctor.

## Small things that help

- Keep a daily routine with time to get dressed, move and rest.
- Stay connected through phone calls, video chats and visitors.
- Set small, achievable goals and notice progress.
- Get outside or sit by a window for natural light.

## Support for caregivers

Caring for a loved one is meaningful and exhausting. Caregivers need breaks, sleep and someone to talk to as well. Asking for help is part of caring well.

## How medical social workers help

As part of home health care, our medical social workers provide short-term counseling, help families plan for the future, and connect patients with community resources such as meal programs, transportation, financial assistance and support groups.

If you or someone you care for is in crisis or thinking about self-harm, call or text **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) or call 911.
