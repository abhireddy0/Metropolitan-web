More care than ever can now be delivered safely at home. Better tools for monitoring, documentation and communication are part of the reason, but the heart of home health care hasn’t changed: a skilled, caring clinician in your home.

## What technology adds

- **Better information at every visit.** Clinicians document assessments electronically, so your whole team sees the same, up-to-date picture.
- **Home monitoring.** Blood pressure cuffs, scales, pulse oximeters and glucose meters help track conditions like heart failure and diabetes between visits.
- **Faster communication.** Nurses can share changes with your physician quickly, so care plans adjust sooner.
- **Staying connected.** Video calls help patients keep in touch with family and, where appropriate, their care team.

## What technology can’t replace

A device can record a number. It takes a nurse to notice that someone seems more tired than last week, that the stairs are getting harder, or that a caregiver is overwhelmed. Those observations, and the trust that grows between patient and clinician, are what keep people safe at home.

## Our approach

At Metropolitan, we use technology where it makes care safer and more efficient, and we keep the focus on the person in front of us. As our values put it: beyond the technology and resources we can offer, caring comes down to human connection.
