More than one in four adults over 65 falls each year, and most falls happen at home. The good news is that small, inexpensive changes make a real difference. Our physical and occupational therapists look for these on every first visit.

## Throughout the house

- Clear walkways of cords, clutter and low furniture.
- Remove loose throw rugs, or secure them with double-sided tape.
- Add bright lighting, especially at the top and bottom of stairs, and night lights between bedroom and bathroom.
- Keep frequently used items between waist and shoulder height, so there is no need to climb or bend low.

## Bathroom

- Install grab bars beside the toilet and inside the shower. Towel bars are not designed to hold body weight.
- Use a non-slip mat in the tub and a shower chair or tub transfer bench.
- A raised toilet seat makes sitting and standing easier after hip or knee surgery.

## Stairs

- Fit sturdy handrails on both sides.
- Mark the edge of each step with contrasting tape if eyesight is a concern.
- Never leave items on the stairs to carry up later.

## Bedroom

- Keep a lamp, phone and glasses within reach of the bed.
- Make sure the bed height lets feet rest flat on the floor when sitting on the edge.

## Beyond the house

Falls are also linked to medications that cause dizziness, poor vision, foot pain and weak legs. Ask your doctor to review medications once a year, keep eye exams up to date, wear supportive shoes indoors, and stay active. A home physical therapy program for strength and balance is one of the most effective ways to prevent falls.

If you’re worried about a loved one’s safety, our therapists can do a home safety assessment as part of a home health plan of care.
