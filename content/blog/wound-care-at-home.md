Surgical incisions, pressure injuries and diabetic foot wounds all heal best with consistent, careful attention. Skilled nurses provide wound care at home every day, and families play an important role between visits.

## The basics

- Wash your hands before and after touching the wound or dressing.
- Follow your nurse’s dressing instructions exactly, including how often to change it.
- Keep the dressing clean and dry, and ask before showering or bathing.
- Don’t apply creams, powders or home remedies unless your nurse or doctor recommends them.

## Support healing from the inside

Wounds need protein, calories and fluids to heal. Include a protein source at each meal, drink enough water unless you’ve been told to limit fluids, and keep blood sugar in your target range if you have diabetes. Smoking slows healing significantly.

## Preventing pressure injuries

For anyone who spends most of the day in bed or a chair, pressure relief is essential. Change position regularly (in bed, typically about every two hours), use pillows to keep heels and bony areas off the mattress, and check the skin daily for redness that doesn’t fade.

## When to call

Contact your nurse or doctor if you notice:

- Redness or warmth spreading around the wound
- Increasing pain, swelling or drainage
- Pus or a bad smell
- Fever or chills
- Edges of an incision coming apart

Our nurses assess and measure wounds, perform dressing changes, teach caregivers, and work with your physician to adjust treatment, so healing stays on track and complications are caught early.
