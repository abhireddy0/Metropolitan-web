Medicare covers home health care for millions of people every year, yet many families don’t realize they qualify until a hospital discharge planner mentions it. Here is how the benefit works.

## Who qualifies

Under Original Medicare (Part A and/or Part B), you can receive home health services when all of the following are true:

- **You are under the care of a doctor**, and your care follows a plan your doctor reviews regularly.
- **Your doctor certifies you need skilled care**: intermittent skilled nursing, physical therapy, speech-language pathology, or continued occupational therapy.
- **You are homebound.** Leaving home takes considerable effort, for example needing a walker, wheelchair or another person’s help. You can still attend medical appointments, religious services or adult day care.
- **You had a face-to-face visit** with your doctor or another qualified provider related to the reason you need home care, within 90 days before or 30 days after care starts.
- **The agency is Medicare-certified.** Metropolitan Home Health Care is.

## What Medicare pays for

If you qualify, you pay **$0** for covered home health services, including part-time skilled nursing, therapy, home health aide visits that support your skilled care, and medical social services. Durable medical equipment such as walkers or hospital beds is covered separately, and you usually pay 20% of the Medicare-approved amount.

## What it does not cover

- 24-hour care at home
- Meal delivery
- Homemaker services such as shopping or cleaning, when that is the only care you need
- Personal care such as bathing or dressing, when that is the only care you need

## How billing works with us

Metropolitan bills Medicare directly and accepts assignment, so you will not receive a bill for covered services. You will receive an Explanation of Benefits after billing. If you have secondary insurance, Medicare bills it directly.
