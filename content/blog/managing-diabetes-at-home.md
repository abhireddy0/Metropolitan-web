Diabetes is managed day by day, at home, through hundreds of small decisions about food, activity and medication. Home health nurses help make those decisions easier and catch problems early.

## Monitoring

Check your blood sugar as often as your doctor recommends and keep a log, or use your meter’s memory, to share at visits. Your doctor will set target ranges for you. Note any readings that are often above or below them.

## Know the signs of low blood sugar

Shakiness, sweating, a fast heartbeat, confusion, irritability and hunger can all mean your blood sugar is too low. Follow the plan your doctor or nurse gave you, which is usually to take fast-acting sugar such as juice or glucose tablets and recheck. Make sure family members know the signs too.

## Food and activity

- Eat regular meals with consistent amounts of carbohydrate.
- Fill half the plate with non-starchy vegetables.
- Choose water over sugary drinks.
- Stay active in ways that are safe for you. Even short walks after meals help.

## Protect your feet

Diabetes can reduce feeling in the feet, so small injuries go unnoticed. Check your feet every day for cuts, blisters, redness or swelling; wear well-fitting shoes; and never walk barefoot. Report any foot wound to your nurse or doctor promptly.

## How home health care helps

Our nurses review blood sugar logs, teach insulin and injection technique, check feet and skin, adjust routines with your physician, and coordinate with a dietitian or podiatry care when needed. The goal is fewer emergencies and more confidence at home.
