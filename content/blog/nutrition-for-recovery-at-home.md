After surgery or illness, the body needs more nutrition, yet appetite often drops. Eating well is one of the simplest ways to support recovery, rebuild strength and prevent setbacks.

## Prioritize protein

Protein helps repair tissue and rebuild muscle. Include a source at every meal and snack: eggs, yoghurt, cheese, fish, chicken, beans, lentils, nut butter or milk.

## When appetite is low

- Eat small meals every two to three hours instead of three large ones.
- Start with the most nourishing part of the plate.
- Add extra calories with olive oil, butter, cheese or milk powder.
- Try smoothies or soups if chewing is tiring.
- Ask your doctor whether a nutrition supplement drink is right for you.

## Stay hydrated

Older adults often feel less thirst. Keep a glass of water within reach and sip throughout the day, unless your doctor has asked you to limit fluids, as with some heart and kidney conditions.

## Follow your medical diet

Heart failure, diabetes and kidney disease each come with specific guidance on salt, sugar or fluid. Our nurses and medical social workers can explain your diet and connect you with meal delivery programs in your area.

## Make mealtimes easier

Prepare and freeze meals in advance, keep healthy snacks ready to eat, and accept help from family and friends. Home health aides and homemakers can also help with shopping and simple meal preparation as part of a care plan.
