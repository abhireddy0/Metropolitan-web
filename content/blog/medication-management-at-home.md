Many people leave the hospital with new prescriptions, changed doses and old bottles still in the cabinet. That is when medication mistakes are most likely. Medication management is one of the most important things our nurses do.

## Start with a complete list

Write down every prescription, over-the-counter medicine, vitamin and supplement, with the dose, time and reason for each. Bring this list, or the bottles themselves, to every appointment. On the first home visit, our nurse compares it with your discharge papers and flags anything that doesn’t match.

## Build a routine

- Use a weekly pill organizer with morning, midday, evening and bedtime compartments.
- Link doses to daily habits, such as breakfast or brushing teeth.
- Set phone alarms or use a talking reminder device.
- Keep a simple checklist on the fridge and tick off each dose.

## Reduce the risk of interactions

- Use one pharmacy where possible, so the pharmacist sees everything you take.
- Ask before starting any new over-the-counter product, including sleep aids and pain relievers.
- Dispose of discontinued medicines safely at a pharmacy drop-off or take-back day.

## Know what to watch for

Call your doctor or nurse if you notice new dizziness, confusion, unusual tiredness, falls, a rash, swelling or stomach upset after a medication change. Never stop a prescribed medicine without talking to your doctor first.

## How our nurses help

Skilled nurses reconcile medications, set up organizers, teach you and your family about each drug, monitor for side effects, and communicate with your physician about any concerns. For many patients, this alone prevents a return trip to the hospital.
