Physical therapy in a clinic is valuable, but for many people, especially right after surgery or a hospital stay, getting there is the hardest part of the day. In-home physical therapy brings the therapist to you.

## Why home is a good place to recover

- **Real-world practice.** You train on your own stairs, chairs, bed and bathroom, the places where independence matters most.
- **Safety built in.** Your therapist sees fall risks first-hand and can recommend changes on the spot.
- **Less exhaustion.** Energy goes into therapy, not into travel.
- **Family involvement.** Caregivers can watch sessions and learn how to help safely.

## What a visit looks like

Your first visit is an evaluation. Your therapist measures strength, range of motion, balance and walking, asks about your goals, and looks at how you move around your home. Together you set goals, such as walking to the kitchen without a walker or managing the stairs to the bedroom.

Later visits typically last 45 to 60 minutes, two to three times a week, and include exercises, gait and balance training, and practice with transfers. You’ll receive a written home exercise program to follow between visits.

## Who benefits most

- People recovering from hip or knee replacement, or other orthopedic surgery
- Stroke survivors and people with Parkinson’s disease or MS
- Anyone who has had a fall or feels unsteady
- People who lost strength during a hospital stay

## Getting started

If you are homebound and your doctor orders therapy, Medicare typically covers home physical therapy in full. Ask your doctor or discharge planner for a home health referral to Metropolitan, or call us and we’ll coordinate with your physician.
